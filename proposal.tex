\begin{abstract}
    Clonal haematopoiesis of indeterminate potential (CHIP) clones that carry TET2
    convert interleukin-1 (IL-1) emergency myelopoiesis into a chronic state. In
    TET2-mutant myeloid cells, glucose uptake is unchanged while carbon is routed into
    pentose-phosphate pathway (PPP) intermediates and UDP-GlcNAc, then OGT and
    ACLY-linked lipid storage. The useful model is allocation of carbon and redox, not
    a simple rise in glycolytic rate. No study has reported a TET2 genotype $\times$
    recombinant IL-1$\beta$ interaction on flux (ECAR, OCR, or $^{13}$C fate). McClatchy
    is haematopoietic stem and progenitor cell (HSPC) RNA; Kim is zymosan myeloid lipid;
    Ni{\~n}o is an HSPC metabolome without IL-1; Flores-Gomez is IL-1$\beta$ training of
    human CD34$^{+}$ cells without TET2 mutants. A multi-head visible neural network (VNN)
    on McClatchy's Tet2 $\times$ IL-1 HSPC gene scores meets $p=0.0001$ for HMP shunt in
    mouse (head~M) and jointly predicts emergency-myelopoiesis rate proxies (head~R);
    an RNA$\to$metabolite prior ranks polar RT-list ions for the LC-MS panel. Human TET2
    $\times$ LPS encodings meet glycolysis, Complex~I, Complex~II, and HMP shunt. We
    hypothesize that TET2 loss changes how IL-1R allocates glucose carbon and redox
    (PPP / UDP-GlcNAc / OGT). Polar LC-MS of cells and medium on the same four arms, a
    clone knockout plus IL-1 added, tests that allocation. Glycolysis and OXPHOS are
    human-LPS and Ni{\~n}o main effects. Lactate, succinate, itaconate, and $\alpha$-KG
    are secondary or negative reads on the mouse IL-1 2$\times$2.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Introduction}

CHIP is an expanded blood clone carrying a myeloid-malignancy driver at variant allele
frequency of at least 2\%, without unexplained cytopenia or frank
neoplasm~\cite{Steensma2015CHIP}. That threshold is a sequencing convention. A common
driver is TET2. Clones accumulate with years. Age-associated myelopoiesis is the myeloid
demand of aged marrow, and interleukin-1 (IL-1) is a main cytokine of that tone. IL-1 on
young wild-type haematopoietic stem and progenitor cells (HSPCs) is emergency
myelopoiesis: an acute pulse~\cite{Pietras2016IL1HSC}. CHIP turns that programme
chronic. Mutant myeloid cells amplify inflammasome tone, the clone expands, wild-type
haematopoietic stem cells (HSCs) exhaust, and inflammaging
rises~\cite{Hajishengallis2026CHIPinflammaging}.

The measurement that is still missing is polar metabolite cargo from TET2-mutant HSPCs
under IL-1. Macrophage IL-1$\beta$ from TET2-deficient clones is already causal for
vascular and metabolic disease. HSPC transcripts, intracellular metabolomes, and myeloid
lipidomes exist. Polar concentrations in HSPC pellets and conditioned medium on a Tet2
$\times$ IL-1 2$\times$2 do not.

We start with Tet2 knockout because TET2 is the metabolic clone and the RNA 2$\times$2
already exists. A visible neural network (VNN) on those gene scores nominates how IL-1
receptor (IL-1R) allocation differs by genotype. The hypothesis is that TET2 loss
changes how IL-1R allocates glucose carbon and redox through the pentose phosphate
pathway (PPP), UDP-GlcNAc, and O-GlcNAc transferase (OGT), not that Tet2 and IL-1 both
raise glycolytic rate. Metabolic-task scores sit downstream of transcript counts and are
not flux.

Polar LC-MS of HSPC cells and medium on the same four arms tests that allocation. The
primary panel is PPP and hexosamine species. Lactate, succinate, itaconate, and
$\alpha$-KG are secondary reads. The four arms are a clone knockout plus IL-1 added,
because HSPCs in a dish do not close the macrophage IL-1 loop.

\section*{Background}

Bone marrow is a shared osteohaematopoietic niche. Haematopoietic stem cells, stroma,
vessels, osteoblasts, osteoclasts, macrophages and T cells exchange cytokines, vesicles
and metabolites. CHIP is associated with incident osteoporosis (hazard ratio 1.44), and
larger DNMT3A clones track with lower bone mineral density; direct skeletal evidence is
stronger for DNMT3A than for
TET2~\cite{He2026ImmuneAgeingSkeletal,Winter2024CHOsteoNiche,Kim2021Dnmt3aOsteoporosis,Wang2024DNMT3Abone}.
Inflammaging is the low-grade inflammatory tone that accumulates with
years~\cite{Franceschi2014Inflammaging}. Frailty is what follows when that tone erodes
reserve.

CHIP and inflammaging reinforce each other. Chronic IL-1$\beta$ and IL-6 injure ordinary
HSCs and favour TET2-mutant clones. The mutant myeloid daughters then raise the same
cytokines. Fuster and colleagues made the myeloid limb causal: TET2-deficient
macrophages oversecrete IL-1$\beta$ through NLRP3 priming and caspase-1 activity,
accelerating atherosclerosis and worsening insulin resistance in ageing and
obesity~\cite{Fuster2017TET2athero,Fuster2020TET2insulin}. Aging bone marrow IL-1
expands Tet2$^{+/-}$ clones; IL-1R blockade slows that
expansion~\cite{Caiado2022Tet2IL1,Caiado2024IL1AgingHSC}. Wild-type HSCs under the same
demand exhaust~\cite{Kovtonyuk2022IL1Microbiome}. Marrow can also be myeloid-biased
without CHIP. Aged CD4 cytotoxic T cells instruct granulopoiesis through CCL5/CCR5 on
HSPCs~\cite{GabandeRodriguez2026CD4CTL}. A four-hour IL-1$\beta$ pulse already trains
human CD34$^{+}$ progenitors toward granulocyte-macrophage output and later
TNF/IL-1$\beta$ recall, with no TET2 arm and no flux in that
assay~\cite{FloresGomez2024IL1training}. Sorted HSPCs do not close the macrophage IL-1
loop, so the 2$\times$2 still adds the cytokine.

McClatchy et al.\ showed that IL-1$\beta$ expands Tet2-loss HSPCs by raising self-renewal
rather than by cutting apoptosis or raising proliferation~\cite{McClatchy2023TET2IL1}.
Tet2-deficient HSPCs resist the IL-1$\beta$-driven demethylation that wild-type cells
undergo at lymphoid and erythroid enhancers. They keep methylation at
pro-differentiation transcription-factor sites and raise self-renewal genes
(\textit{Erg}, \textit{Meis1}, \textit{Egr1}) plus myeloid-bias programmes. The
transcriptional 2$\times$2 (wild-type versus Tet2-knockout, with or without IL-1$\beta$)
shows inflammatory, ageing-like, and AP-1 programmes amplified in Tet2-knockout HSCs.
That dataset is RNA and DNA methylation, not metabolite concentrations or export.

Human TET2-edited HSPCs split by compartment. Mutant HSPCs silence AP-1 factors (FOS,
JUN) and blunt the transcriptional response to LPS, including metabolic and growth
programmes~\cite{HuergaEncabo2026TET2inflammation}. Their monocyte/macrophage daughters
become hyperinflammatory and enrich for \textit{IL1B}-positive monocytes. That split
explains how TET2-mutant clonal haematopoiesis can keep stemness in HSPCs while raising
inflammation through myeloid progeny. The datasets are RNA-seq and epigenomic. They do
not report metabolites from HSPCs or conditioned medium.

Ni{\~n}o et al.\ mapped the HSPC-intrinsic metabolome of Tet2
loss~\cite{Nino2026TETmetabolism}. Tet2-deficient HSPCs overexpress glycolysis and
oxidative phosphorylation (OXPHOS) genes. Seahorse assays show higher maximal oxygen
consumption and extracellular acidification. Stable-isotope tracing increases
glucose-derived labelling of lactate, citrate, malate, fumarate, glutamate,
acetyl-carnitine, and glutathione, with higher ATP, NAD$^{+}$/NADH, and a larger
mitochondrial network. Redox stays normal because glutathione pools expand and
glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PD) in the PPP supplies NADPH. Compound G6PD loss
raises reactive oxygen species and selectively cuts Tet2-deficient HSPC fitness. Human
CH and CCUS HSPCs show the same oxidative bias. That metabolome has no IL-1 arm and does
not measure export.

Kim, Hergott, Ebert and colleagues mapped TET2 metabolism from the myeloid
side~\cite{Kim2026TET2metabolism}. An in vivo genome-wide CRISPR screen in wild-type and
Tet2-knockout HSPCs, read out in zymosan peritonitis, recovered \textit{Ogt} and
\textit{Acly} as Tet2-selective inflammation nodes. Tet2 restrains OGT. Loss raises
O-GlcNAc, H3K4me3 at lipid-anabolic loci, and ATP-citrate lyase (ACLY) activity. Glucose
uptake is unchanged. $^{13}$C-glucose tracing sends more carbon into PPP intermediates
and UDP-GlcNAc. Neutrophils, bone-marrow-derived macrophages (BMDMs), and human
CD34$^{+}$-derived macrophages accumulate di- and triacylglycerols and cholesterol
esters. Polar profiling of Tet2-knockout BMDMs ranked glycerophospholipid metabolism,
not lactate, succinate, itaconate, or $\alpha$-KG export. ACLY inhibition blunts
Tet2-knockout hyperinflammation, including \textit{Il1b}. That is myeloid lipid
anabolism under innate challenge, not FACS-sorted HSPC polar LC-MS on a Tet2 $\times$
IL-1 2$\times$2, and not conditioned-medium cargo.

Lalioti et al.\ ran targeted polar metabolomics on about 3{,}000 human
lineage-negative CD34-positive CD38-negative marrow HSPCs against CD38-positive
progenitors~\cite{Lalioti2025HSCmetabolome}. Across 17 young donors, 73 metabolites
passed quality control and 47 differed. HSPCs had lower TCA-cycle metabolites,
nucleotides and many amino acids, and higher NAD and choline. That is a more quiescent
polar profile than progenitors. Choline fell with age and further in AML. Adding
choline back fuelled lipid production and stemness in culture. The work covers
differentiation, ageing and leukaemia. It has no cytokine challenge and no CHIP.
DeVilbiss, Agathocleous and colleagues detected about 160 metabolites from 10{,}000
murine HSCs by HILIC Orbitrap MS~\cite{DeVilbiss2021HSCmetabolome}.

Zeng et al.\ mapped young versus aged mouse blood-cell metabolomes. Young HSPCs enrich
purine, pyrimidine and retinol metabolism. Aged HSPCs enrich glutamate and
sphingolipid metabolism. Uridine can restore aged HSC self-renewal through FoxO and
lower inflammation~\cite{Zeng2024UridineHSC}. Mitroulis et al.\ showed that
$\beta$-glucan trained immunity changes HSPCs through IL-1$\beta$ and GM-CSF, with
glycolysis and cholesterol-biosynthesis changes that persist into a later challenge.
Isolated LSK cells given IL-1$\beta$ for 24\,h raise
ECAR~\cite{Mitroulis2018TrainedImmunity}. Sezaki et al.\ review how infection and
inflammation can leave memory in HSCs~\cite{Sezaki2020HSPCinflammation}. Whether IL-1
produces a distinct acute-to-chronic metabolic trajectory in aged and CHIP-mutant
HSPCs, and whether that trajectory predicts clone fitness and lineage output, is not
tested there.

Extracellular succinate is already a bone-niche ligand. It acts through SUCNR1 on
osteoclast-lineage cells, drives NF-$\kappa$B, and stimulates osteoclastogenesis. SUCNR1
blockade or knockout removes the
effect~\cite{Guo2017Succinate}. In diabetic marrow, stromal-cell succinate rose
24-fold, and succinate administration caused bone loss. HSPC SUCNR1 is a second
receiver. It restricts haematopoiesis, so succinate can expand HSPCs when the receptor
is low~\cite{Cuminetti2026SUCNR1}. If Tet2-mutant HSPCs export succinate under IL-1,
those two receptors are the tests of whether the cargo is sensed. Medium succinate that
does not move either assay leaves the niche claim untested.

\section*{Approach}

We first trained a metabolic visible neural network (VNN) on scCellFie gene scores for
glycolysis, oxidative phosphorylation/TCA, and pentose-phosphate (PPP) tasks in those
GSE209994 marker-HSPCs (WT/Tet2 $\times$ vehicle/IL-1, 8
libraries)~\cite{McClatchy2023TET2IL1,Armingol2025scCellFie}. A VNN is a neural net
whose layers follow a knowledge graph. Mouse and human samples were trained separately for 100 epochs on GPU, reconstructing every cell
in that species' 2$\times$2. The VNN reconstructs each cell's gene scores through
that hierarchy, then the task encodings are frozen. The final figure is the factorial
interaction map, one 2$\times$2 per metabolic task from the frozen encodings
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mice}). The statistic is
$(\mathrm{Tet2_{IL1}}-\mathrm{WT_{IL1}})-(\mathrm{Tet2_{vehicle}}-\mathrm{WT_{vehicle}})$.
Colour is the four-arm mean, centered and scaled so the largest absolute arm is 1
representing the relative mean of the interaction. Panel titles are metabolic tasks and
match the gene-cloud labels. Those panels are VNN encodings of treatment within
genotype. Relative mean can look strong when the encodings are small. Glycolysis has
the most positive Tet2/IL-1 cell in that scaled map. Complex~I has the most negative.
Scaled colour is not the test. The mouse test that survives Bonferroni is HMP shunt.

After freeze, task encodings form a metabolic embedding $X_{\mathrm{vnn}}$ (optionally
concatenated with EM gene-set proxies for Myc, OXPHOS, and glutamine that never enter
the VNN loss). Diffusion pseudotime on that embedding, rooted at the nearest cell to the
control-arm centroid (WT$\cdot$vehicle in mouse; WT$\cdot$CTRL in human), traces
metabolic EM progression inside HSPCs. Soft branch occupancy (stem / myeloid / MegE /
lymphoid) is scored from marker modules in the same frozen space. Per-arm GAMs then fit
each task against $s(\mathrm{pseudotime})$ with FDR control
(Fig.~\ref{fig:mice-em}). Genotype and IL-1 never enter the VNN loss. Polar RT-list ions
are ranked from differential scCellFie reaction activity linked through BiGG
metabolites (Table~\ref{tab:ions}), not from hardcoded task hints.

The $p$ value under each panel is $(1+k)/10000$ from 9{,}999 gene shuffles, where $k$ is
how many random gene sets of the same size were at least as extreme. Bonferroni over
seven mouse tasks or eight human tasks is about $0.006$ to $0.007$; 9{,}999 shuffles put
the floor at $0.0001$, below that cutoff. Bold titles mark $p<0.05$.
Human GSE285379 FACS-HSPCs (TET2 $\times$ LPS, 4 libraries) use a separate VNN of the
same hierarchy (Fig.~\ref{fig:human})~\cite{HuergaEncabo2023TET2neutrophil}. The
competitive mean test meets $p<0.05$ for glycolysis, Complex~I and Complex~II (all
$p=0.0001$), HMP shunt ($p=0.0014$), Krebs-cycle NADH generation ($p=0.006$), and ATP
regeneration from glucose ($p=0.043$). Bonferroni $0.05/8$ keeps the first five except
ATP regeneration. Pyruvate decarboxylation ($p=0.65$) and ribose-5-phosphate
($p=0.89$) do not meet $p<0.05$. Mouse HSPCs meet $p<0.05$ for HMP shunt ($p=0.0001$).
The other six mouse tasks do not, including glycolysis ($p=0.48$) and Complex~I
($p=0.34$). Mouse IL-1 therefore nominates PPP allocation. Glycolysis and OXPHOS stay
with the human LPS map and with the HSPC metabolome that had no IL-1. That G6PD/PPP
fitness map is why HMP shunt is worth a cheap G6PD assay. The permutation did not test
G6PD activity.

From the mouse encodings, the RT-list prior ranks 6-phosphogluconate, NADPH, erythrose-4-phosphate,
and NADP$^{+}$ as the top primary ions (matched to HMP/ribose tasks), with succinate,
$\alpha$-KG, and lactate as secondary reads (Table~\ref{tab:ions}). That ranking feeds
the cheap-assay$\to$LC-MS panel below; it is not flux.

Gene embedding clouds are the interpretability for that map (Fig.~\ref{fig:mice-genes},
Fig.~\ref{fig:human-genes}). Each point is one task gene. $x$ is the challenge main on
frozen gene embeddings. $y$ is the same interaction as the 2$\times$2 panels. Labels
collapse Complex~I subunits to Nduf / mt-Nd. Most effects coincide, with Complex~I as a
pile at the origin. Sdha and Ogdh map to succinate and $\alpha$-KG, Pkm maps to lactate,
and Tkt maps to NADPH and pentose. Those embeddings name genes off the pile. They are
not the permutation test. Challenge is not the same (IL-1 versus LPS), so the maps are not
stacked. These libraries contain HSPCs only, which is why the LC-MS stays in HSPCs.

\section*{Proposed experiment}

The wet lab is the same four arms: isolate HSPCs, knock out TET2, and add IL-1 or
vehicle. Polar LC-MS of cells and medium is the readout. The genotype $\times$
IL-1$\beta$ term on flux is what would refute or support the allocation hypothesis.
Colony assays, 5-hydroxymethylcytosine, and G6PD or Seahorse come first. They test
whether the cultures recapture myeloid output, TET2 catalysis, and the published
metabolic shift before the LC-MS spend, and whether head~R rate proxies move.

The primary polar panel follows the mouse HMP-shunt encoding and the RT-list ranking
(Table~\ref{tab:ions}): 6-phosphogluconate, NADPH/NADP$^{+}$, erythrose-4-phosphate,
sedoheptulose-7-phosphate, fructose-6-phosphate, and UDP-GlcNAc. Lactate, succinate,
itaconate, and $\alpha$-KG are pre-specified secondary or negative reads. If they move
here, that is a Tet2 main effect of the intracellular kind, not the mouse IL-1
interaction. If medium succinate rises, a SUCNR1 reporter or an osteoclast/HSPC
co-culture fed that medium tests whether the cargo is sensed. If LC-MS shows an
$\alpha$-KG/succinate shift and 5hmC did not move at the cheap step, the metabolites are
not reaching catalysis.

Matched RNA-seq of the same cultures would later feed a multi-omics factor model and a
gene-regulatory network. Those analyses are not required to start the cheap assays or
the LC-MS. Fracture-union prediction and IL-1$\beta$ blockade remain cohort questions.

\section*{Conclusion}

TET2 loss is hypothesized to change how IL-1R allocates glucose carbon and redox through
PPP, UDP-GlcNAc, and OGT, rather than to raise glycolytic rate on both perturbations.
Mouse VNN encodings meet that interaction for HMP shunt ($p=0.0001$). Human TET2
$\times$ LPS encodings meet glycolysis, both OXPHOS complexes, and HMP shunt; the
challenges differ, so the maps are not stacked. Polar LC-MS of cells and medium on the
four arms tests the allocation panel. Lactate, succinate, itaconate, and $\alpha$-KG stay
secondary. Cheap CFU, 5hmC, and G6PD or Seahorse assays come first. A SUCNR1 assay
follows only if medium succinate moves.
